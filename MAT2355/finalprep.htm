How to succeed in the final exam:

  • The final exam is based on all of the material that was covered in class. For further information refer to the syllabus on the course webpage and scanned notes.
  • You should know all of the definitions that are mentioned in class. Some examples of definitions are: Cauchy sequences, supremum of a set of real numbers, infimum of a set of real numbers, accumulation point of a sequence, limsup and liminf, absolute convergence of a series, pointwise convergence and uniform convergence of sequences of functions, uniform continuity, upper and lower Darboux sums, upper and lower integral, norm on Rn, neighborhood, open set, closed set, compact set.
  • As a reference for definitions, you can use the scanned class notes that are available on Virtual Campus. In the exam, if you are asked to write a definition, you should write the definition as precisely as possible. Your answer does not have to be "word for word" the same as the one given in the notes. However, your answer should be accurate. If you state a definition vaguely, you will lose points.
  • You should review the assignments and the solutions of the questions solved in the DGD (available on Virtual Campus). Study the posted solutions and compare them to your own solutions. Some of the assignment and DGD questions might appear on the final exam.
  • It is a very good idea to work on the Recommended Exercises For Further Practice which are posted on the Syllabus webpage. Although a small number of them are probably too hard to be on the exam, they will test your knowledge and your ablility to use the techniques you have learned in this course.
  • In the exam, solving some of the questions may require application of some theorems. In your solutions, you should clearly indicate which theorem you are using (by briefly mentioning the statement of the theorem, or by giving its title, e.g., "Bolzano—Weierstrass Theorem", or "The Mean Value Theorem").
  • Write all of the necessary details of your proofs. For example, if you need to use induction, do not simply write "it follows by induction". You should explain the details of the induction argument.
  • In the exam, you will be asked to prove two theorems. You should know the proofs of the following list of theorems. Numbers refer to statement numbering in the electronic version of the textbook. For your convenience, I have added a brief description of each statement.

    Theorem III.1.3   (every convergent sequence is bounded).
    Theorem III.5.10   (a continuous function on a closed interval is integrable).
    Theorem III.5.17   (mean value theorem).
    Theorem IV.1.14   (the complement of an open set is closed and the complement of a closed set is open).
    Theorem IV.1.19   (only one direction: if K is a bounded and closed subset of Rn, then K is compact).
    Theorem IV.2.8   (Characterization of continuity in terms of open and closed sets).

Good luck!
