How to succeed in the midterm exam:

  • The midterm exam is based on all of the material that was covered in class before the reading week.
  • You should know all of the definitions that are mentioned in class. As a reference, you can use the class notes that have been uploaded on Virtual Campus. In the exam, if you are asked to write a definition, you should write the definition as precisely as possible. This does not mean that your definition should be word for word the same as the one given in the notes. It means that your definition should be accurate. If you state a definition vaguely, you will lose points.
  • In the exam, you may either directly or indirectly be asked to give examples and non-examples. When you study the definitions, think of examples (e.g., an example of a non-abelian group with 6 elements) and non-examples (e.g., a group which is abelian but not cyclic). The examples mentioned in class might be helpful.
  • You should review the assignments. Study the posted solutions and compare them to your own solutions. After you are done with reviewing the assignments, it is a very good idea to work on the recommended exercises which are indicated on the course webpage. These questions test your knowledge and your ablility to use the techniques you have learned in this course.
  • You should know the statements of all of the theorems that are mentioned in class. Again, you can use the uploaded notes as a reference. In the exam, solving some of the questions may require application of some theorems. Whenever you are using a theorem to solve a question, you should clearly indicate which theorem you are using (e.g., by briefly mentioning the statement of the theorem).
  • In the exam, you will be asked to write the proofs of two theorems. You should know the proofs of the following theorems from the posted course notes:

    § 3.1 : Theorem 7.
    § 3.3 : Theorem 3, Theorem 4, Theorem 6.
    § 3.4 : Theorem 4.
    § 4.1 : Theorem 5, Theorem 9.
    § 4.2 : Theorem 5.

Good luck!
