General Description of the Course

Lie algebras and their representations are one of the areas of active research in pure mathematics. They play a key role in connecting diverse areas of research, such as number theory, category theory, harmonic analysis, and algebraic combinatorics. In addition, they are frequently encountered in applied mathematics and beyond, e.g. quantum information, cryptography, and particle physics. This course aims to introduce the most fundamental results from the theory of Lie algebras, their classification, and their representations. In my view, these are what every graduate student in pure mathematics should know ...

Advanced undergraduate students who are interested in doing a summer research project (USRA, UROP, Honours project, etc.), or an M.Sc. in the field of algebra, will find this course very useful and interesting.

Official Course Description

Structure of solvable, nilpotent and semisimple finite dimensional Lie algebras.

Course Text

The lectures are essentially based on the following text:

  • Karin Erdmann, Mark J. Wildon, Introduction to Lie Algebras, Springer, 2006.

This book is the main text for students enrolled in either MAT4142 or MAT5143. You can download it from the publisher's website through the university proxies.

The additional reading material for students enrolled in MAT5143 might be selected from the following books:

  • Theodor Bröcker, Tammo tom Dieck, Representations of Compact Lie Groups, Springer, 1985.
  • Roger W. Carter, Lie Algebras of Finite and Affine Type, Cambridge, 2005.
